## 【正文】

### 一、普賢王如來六空概述

「普賢王如來六空」（Six Spaces of Samantabhadra），藏語為「昆土贊波隆竹」（kun tu bzang po klong drug），亦譯作「普賢王如來六界」，是在大圓滿（Dzogchen）傳承中，顯示本初狀態六種面向的一項核心教法。這六種「空」與「六字心咒」完全同義，即：「a ah ha sha sa ma」六字，此六字被視為輪迴六道之化身佛——六應身（六報身／六應化身）之音節形態。

這六字對應於六道輪迴，常被稱為「六趣」（六 loka），然而嚴格說，梵語應為「gati」。六趣為：天（deva）、阿修羅（asura）、人（manuṣya, human）、畜生、餓鬼、地獄眾生，並同時對應六識：見（視覺）、聞（聽覺）、嗅（嗅覺）、嘗（味覺）、觸（觸覺），以及意識之了別。在大圓滿修持中，透過六識與此六字相接觸，便成為「六種解脫」之因，使眾生得以淨除業力條件，並證得自性本然：

- 見解脫（mthong.grol）：透過觀看這些字形或壇城。
- 聞解脫（thos.grol）：透過聽聞此心咒之聲。
- 嗅解脫（dri.grol）：透過嗅聞由咒語加持之香氣。
- 味解脫（myong.drol）：透過食用被咒語加持的飲食或水。
- 觸解脫（btags.grol）：透過身體觸碰與咒語相關的表徵物。
- 念解脫（dran.grol）：透過憶念、思惟此咒語之義。

### 二、六空密續與六空之舞

《普賢王如來六空續》（kun tu bzang po klong drug pa'i rgyud）是寧瑪派「十七續」之一，教導如何防止於六道再生、如何淨化六道，並顯現自顯清淨剎土。此一教法常透過「普賢王如來六空之舞」表現出來，也是一種身語意的動中修行，旨在逆轉輪迴運動，使能量和諧，並重新發現自身的本然狀態。

### 三、六字與六種解脫（grol ba drug ldan）

在大圓滿教法中，〈金剛之歌〉與「六字」（Six Syllables），都是成辦「六種解脫」（grol ba drug ldan）的強力法門。所謂六種解脫，即是指六識與境相接觸的方式：

由於我們的六識——見、聞、嗅、嘗、觸與意識之了別——與此六字相接觸，能夠成為趨向解脫的善因緣。  

普賢王如來六字，亦稱為「法性之聲」（dharmatā 之聲，chos nyid），因為這些聲音是存在實際狀態本然之聲，即「a ah ha sha sa ma」。

### 四、六種解脫之具體形態

六種解脫之一，是較為人所知的「聞解脫」（thödrol, thos grol），在著名的《中陰聞解脫》（Bardo Thödrol）中，以誦讀於臨終者耳邊而聞名。如同這些「聞解脫」之經文與咒語能成辦聞而解脫的因，同樣地，也有「觸解脫」或「佩戴解脫」（btags grol）。例如，我們會設法將「塔卓」（tagdrol，佩觸解脫物）放在臨終者的身體上，尤其對於並非行者（修行者）之人而言，令其身體能與此接觸。

至於「見解脫」（mthong grol），則是將六字或〈金剛之歌〉寫出來，或製作為莊嚴裝飾、小壇城等，安置於眾人得以見聞之處。即便他們並不理解或不感興趣，六字的力量仍然為其種下解脫的善因。「造作因緣」並非意指人們一見到那些字母就立刻獲得智慧或成佛，而是指：原本沒有因緣進入正道、不能接受與隨順教法之人，藉此而具有那樣的可能性。

另一種類型是「嗅解脫」（dri grol），乃是以此心咒加持特別的香與氣味，使得眾生只要嗅聞到這種氣味，就能成就善因。

一種重要方便為「味解脫」（myong grol）。其中會和合一百零八種成分，其中某些成分極為稀有且難以尋覓。除了調製此類藥物之外，尚須修持一至兩週，持咒加持，直至顯現某些修持之徵相，此時「味解脫」（nyongdrol, nyong grol）才具足力量。嚐到這一類「味解脫」之藥，便可種下殊勝善因。

對大圓滿行者而言，最重要的是「念解脫」（dran drol）。所謂意識之了別，不只是思考與評判，而是安住於對聲音真實義理的覺知與記持之中。因而，唱誦並專心於六字或〈金剛之歌〉，能增上行者的明晰。同時，這也是增上自身修持、迴向與諸種事業力用的方法。

### 五、南開諾布仁波切之開示

在此方面，卻嘉南開諾布仁波切（Chögyal Namkhai Norbu）如是說：

> 「雖然行者不需要再造作解脫之因，因為我們已經在隨順教法與從事修持，本來就具有解脫之因，但若運用任何一種『六種解脫』，都能大大幫助我們增上明晰。基於此緣故，行者也會運用這些方法。」

Six Spaces of Samantabhadra    
The Six Spaces of Samantabhadra, Kuntuzangpo Longdrug (kun tu bzang po klong drug), also known as the Six Spheres of Samantabhadra, are a core teaching in the Dzogchen tradition representing the six aspects of the primordial state.  These spaces are synonymous with the Six Syllables mantra: a ah ha sha sa ma, which are considered the syllabic form of the nirmanakāyas of the six realms of existence.     
These six syllables correspond to the six realms of transmigration, which are known as six lokas even though the Sankrit term should be gati: deva, asura, manuṣya or human, animal, hungry ghost, and hell beings and the six senses of seeing, hearing, smelling, tasting, touching, and mental perception.  In dzogchen practice, contact with these syllables through the six senses creates the cause for the six liberations, allowing beings to purify their karmic conditions and realize their true nature:    
Liberation through seeing (mthong.grol): Viewing written forms or mandalas of the syllables.     
Liberation through hearing (thos.grol): Hearing the sounds of the mantra.     
Liberation through smell (dri.grol): Perceiving scents empowered by the mantra.     
Liberation through taste (myong.drol): Consuming food or water touched by the mantra.     
Liberation through touch (btags.grol): Physical contact with the mantra's representation.     
Liberation through mind (dran.grol): Remembering or contemplating the mantra's meaning.     
The Tantra of the Six Spheres of Samantabhadra (kun tu bzang po klong drug pa'i rgyud) is one of the Seventeen Tantras of the Nyingma school and teaches how to prevent rebirth in the six realms, purify them, and manifest the pure realms of self-display.  This practice is often embodied in the Dance of the Six Spaces of Samantabhadra, a movement practice designed to harmonize energy and rediscover one's natural condition by reversing the movement of samsara.     
In the Dzogchen teachings, both the Song of the Vajra and the Six Syllables, are a powerful means for the six kinds of liberation, Drolwa Drugden (grol ba drug ldan). The six kinds of liberation refer to our six senses and to their contact with sense objects. This is why contact between our six senses - seeing, hearing, smelling, tasting, touching and mental perception - and these six syllables have the potentiality to create a virtuous cause for gaining liberation.     
The Six Syllables of Samantabhadra also known as the 'sounds of dharmatā (chos nyid)' as they are the natural sounds of the actual condition of existence, which are a ah ha sha sa ma.     
One of the six liberations is the more well-known liberation through hearing or thödrol (thos grol), which is known from the famous Bardo Thödrol texts read for the dying. Just like the thödrol texts and mantras that create a cause for liberation through hearing, there is also liberation through touch or by wearing (btags grol). For instance, we try to place tagdrol in contact with the physical body of those who are dying, especially those who are not practitioners.     
As for the liberation through seeing (mthong grol) it is writing the six syllables or the Song of the Vajra or arranging them as ornaments or small mandalas while exposing them at places where people can see them. Although they may not understand or be interested in these writings, the power of the six syllables creates a good cause for them to gain liberation. 'Making a cause' does not mean that upon seeing those letters people immediately receive wisdom or enlightenment, but rather those who do not have the cause for being on a path, for receiving and following the teaching, then have that possibility.     
Another kind is liberation through smelling (dri grol) where special incense and scents are empowered with this mantra so that by perceiving that odor or scent a good cause is created.    
An important means of liberation is liberation through taste (myong grol), where one hundred and eight ingredients are integrated; some of those ingredients are very rare and not easy to find at all. In addition to preparing that medicine, one has to practice for one or two weeks empowering it until some signs of the practice manifest and the nyongdrol becomes powerful. Tasting this kind of nyongdrol can make a good cause.     
For a dzogchen practitioner the most important is liberation through mental peception (dran drol); mental perception is not just thinking and judging but remaining present and aware in the recollection of the actual meaning of the sound. Thus, by singing and concentrating on the Six Syllables or the Song of the Vajra the practitioner's clarity increases. At the same time it is a method for empowering one's practice, dedication or activities.     
In this respect Chögyal Namkhai Norbu states: "Although practitioners don’t need to create a cause of liberation, because we already have a cause since we are already following teaching and doing practice, if we use any kind of these Six Liberations, it greatly helps to increase our clarity. For that reason practitioners also make use of them."
