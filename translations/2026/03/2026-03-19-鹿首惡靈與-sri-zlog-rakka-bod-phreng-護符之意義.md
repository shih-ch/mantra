## 【正文】

### 標題

Sitsen Dogpa——秋吉林巴（Chokgyur Lingpa）所傳  
鹿首死神惡靈所致違緣與障礙之護符

---

近期，Naldjor 張貼了一則關於「執持護身戰矛之紅色鹿首岑靈（tsen）」的內容。隨後，有許多人回覆，談到這個或那個紅色岑靈，和／或在美國等地出現的鹿首靈眾等。由此，或許讓人產生一種印象：凡是那些鹿首或牡鹿首的靈，尤其是身色為紅者，全都是護持的力量。

事實恰恰相反：在藏語中，有一類強力的岑靈（btsan）以及稱為「si（sri）」或「damsi（dam sri）」的惡鬼魔眾，梵語則稱為「rākṣasa（羅剎）」，其類別極多——其中許多是人身、鹿頭或羊頭等動物頭面。最常在修法儀軌（sādhana）中被提及的是「damsi」一類，它們起源於昔日破壞自己三昧耶（samaya）誓約的修行人。所以，對於鹿首之靈，最好還是多加留意。

---

有一則貼文提供了如下解說：

在金剛乘（Vajrayāna）佛教傳統中，特別是在喜馬拉雅地區，如西藏、上金納爾（Upper Kinnaur）、不丹、尼泊爾、拉達克與斯必提（Spiti）一帶，人們常使用神聖的護符或符 amulet 來作為靈性防護，其中一種被稱為「Sri Zlog Rakka Bod Phreng」（藏文：སྲི་ཟློག་རཱཀྐ་རྦོད་ཕྲེང་）。

這種強力的護符，被認為能夠驅遣惡意之勢力（སྲི་，sri）、黑魔法、因鬼神干擾所致之疾病，以及那些不可見卻阻礙修行道程的違緣障礙。

此名稱本身包含深義：「Sri Zlog」（སྲི་ཟློག་）指的是「遣除、驅逆」有害影響，其中「sri」代表有害的能量或鬼靈，「zlog」則是轉開、遣散之意。「Rakka」（རཱཀྐ་）源自梵語 rakṣa，意為保護、守護，常用於密續語境，召請忿怒護法尊或咒力。「Bod」（རྦོད་）在此表示驅散、放逐、趕走；「Phreng」（ཕྲེང་）是串、鬘、念珠之意，隱含一串護符咒語或種子字（ཡི་གེ་མཚན་པོ་，yi ge mtshan po），如同神聖之鏈一樣依序排列。

此類護符通常寫在薄紙捲軸上，以聖墨書寫其上，刻寫強力陀羅尼、種子字（ཡི་གེ་ཡིག་སྣོན་）、對忿怒本尊之召請（མགོན་པོ་ཁུར་འཇིགས་པ་），以及諸如金剛網、火圈或本尊壇城（maṇḍala）等護符圖像（སྒྲུབ་བྱེད་རིས་པ་）。之後再將其卷起、包裹，封入護袋（རྡོག་པ་，rdog pa）中，佩戴在頸項，或繫於門楣、安置於佛塔之上，或懸掛於聖樹之間。

在金剛乘之中，觀想與持咒被視為淨化業力、轉化知覺的直接方法，這類「phreng」（咒鬘／字鬘）就不僅僅是象徵，而是一個活生生的加持（བྱིན་རླབས་，byin rlabs）場域，直接連結於上師之灌頂（དབང་，dbang）、本尊（yidam）事業之運作（ཡི་དམ་ལས་，yi dam las），以及護法神（dharmapāla，ཆོས་སྐྱོང་，chos skyong）的守護。此類護符的使用有其古老根源，上溯至印度的密教時期；直到 2025 年的今日，它們仍廣泛流通，有時以數位列印方式製作，並以繁複的儀式進行加持後，再於宗教節慶（སྲས་སྒོ་ལོ༹་གསར་（待考））或寺院中的大法會（སྒྲུབ་ཆེན་，drubchen）期間分發。  

因此，「Sri Zlog Rakka Bod Phreng」可說是經教威力、象徵防護與活生生佛法力量的神聖結合，體現了一個原則：當覺知與慈悲經由咒語與誓願而被加持、聖化之時，便成為對治一切無明與傷害的鎧甲。

## 【疑點/勘誤】

1. 原文「Red Dear-Headed Tsen Spirit」  
   - 疑點：應為「Red Deer-Headed Tsen Spirit」；dear 應為 deer 之誤寫。  
   - 建議：保留為「紅色鹿首岑靈」。

2. 「poeple」  
   - 疑點：拼寫錯誤。  
   - 正常應為：people。

3. 「Dear-headed spirits」  
   - 疑點：同樣 deer/dear 混用。  
   - 建議：一律理解為「鹿首」。

4. 「Protective Battle-lance Wielding Red Dear-Headed Tsen Spirit」  
   - 疑點：語序與大小寫略顯不規則。  
   - 建議理解：一位「執戰矛之紅色鹿首守護岑靈」。

5. 藏文「སྲས་སྒོ་ལོ༹་གསར་」  
   - 疑點：中間的「ོ༹」可能為輸入或字體疊碼錯誤，應為「སྲས་སྒོ་ལོ་གསར་」或另有正規拼寫（待考）。

Sitsen Dogpa - Choggyur Lingpa's Protective Charm for Reversing Hindrances and Obstacles Caused by Deer-Headed Si Spirits    
Recently, the Protective Battle-lance Wielding Red Dear-Headed Tsen Spirit has been posted by Naldjor. Numerous poeple answered about this and that red-colored tsen spirit or/and dear-headed spirits in US and so on. Thus, there might have been the impression that all those dear- or stag-headed spirits and especially those with a red body color are protective forces. On the contrary, there are powerful tsen spirits (btsan) and demonic forces known as si (sri) or damsi (dam sri) in Tibetan and rākṣasa in Sanskrit, which are of many kinds - many have a human body with the head of an animal such as a deer or sheep. Most often in sadhanas the damsi spirits are mentioned, who originate from practitioners who previously broke their samaya commitments. So better watch out for deer-headed spirits.    
One post offered the following explanation:     
In the Vajrayāna Buddhist tradition, especially across the Himalayan regions such as Tibet, Upper Kinnaur, Bhutan, Nepal, Ladakh, and Spiti, sacred amulets or talismans are often used for spiritual protection, one of which is known as "Sri Zlog Rakka Bod Phreng" (སྲི་ཟློག་རཱཀྐ་རྦོད་ཕྲེང་).     
This powerful protective item is believed to ward off malevolent forces (སྲི་, sri), black magic, illness caused by spirit interference, and unseen obstacles that obstruct the path of spiritual progress.     
The term itself carries deep meaning: “Sri Zlog” (སྲི་ཟློག་) refers to the act of repelling or averting harmful influences, where "sri" stands for harmful energies or spirits, and "zlog" means to turn away or dispel. "Rakka" (རཱཀྐ་) is derived from the Sanskrit rakṣa, meaning protection or guarding, often used in tantric contexts to invoke fierce protector deities or mantra energies. "Bod" (རྦོད་) here signifies to scatter, banish, or drive out, while "Phreng" (ཕྲེང་) means garland or rosary, implying a string of protective mantras or seed syllables (ཡི་གེ་མཚན་པོ་, yi ge mtshan po), arranged in a sequence like a sacred chain.     
Such talismans are commonly written on thin paper scrolls using sacred ink, inscribed with powerful dhāraṇīs, seed syllables (ཡི་གེ་ཡིག་སྣོན་), invocations to wrathful deities (མགོན་པོ་ཁུར་འཇིགས་པ་), and protective diagrams (སྒྲུབ་བྱེད་རིས་པ་) like vajra nets, fire circles, or deity mandalas, and are then wrapped and sealed into pouches (རྡོག་པ་, rdog pa) worn around the neck, tied at doorways, placed on stupas, or hung on sacred trees.     
In Vajrayāna, where visualization and mantra recitation are seen as direct methods to purify karma and transform perception, such a phreng is more than just a symbol—it is a living field of blessing energy (བྱིན་རླབས་, byin rlabs), directly linked to the guru’s empowerment (དབང་, dbang), the yidam’s activity (ཡི་དམ་ལས་, yi dam las), and the protection of dharmapālas (ཆོས་སྐྱོང་, chos skyong). The use of such amulets has ancient roots, going back to India’s tantric period, and continues to be widespread in 2025, where they are sometimes digitally printed or consecrated with elaborate rituals before being distributed during religious festivals (སྲས་སྒོ་ལོ་གསར་) or drupchens (སྒྲུབ་ཆེན་) in monasteries. The Sri Zlog Rakka Bod Phreng is thus a sacred blend of scriptural power, symbolic protection, and the living force of the Dharma, embodying the principle that awareness and compassion, when consecrated through mantra and intention, become armor against all forms of ignorance and harm.
