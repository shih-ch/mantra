## 長壽——與壽命與呼吸相關的密續與醫學細脈

依據藏傳密續各大法系以及傳統藏醫學（TTM），有各種密續與醫學體系闡明極為微細的細脈與毛細脈，它們遍佈並瀰漫整個人身，如同極為精細的細小支脈網絡（yang phran rtsa’i lus kyi dra bar ‘brel tshul）。這些脈網源自三條主要細脈，經由各輪／脈輪（cakra）的連結細脈而分出，互相銜接最微細的支脈，如同極細密的網。就此而言，《四部醫典》及其註釋《青琉璃》將與一個人的長壽或壽命（tshe）相關的「三種行相與方式」作如下說明：

1. 與「壽命細脈」（tshe’i rtsa）有關的行相與方式：在一個月諸日之中，以種子字的形態，遍佈整個身體與頭部（yi ge’i rnam pa nya stong tshe mgo lus thams cad du khyab pa）。

2. 與「五種微細元素」相關之氣（風力）的行相與方式：此即與自身呼吸一同運行的生命氣（‘byung ba’i rlung dbugs dang ‘grogs te rgyu ba）。

3. 與「自身根本守護能量」相關的行相與方式：在一個月諸日之中，以種子字的形態，於身體與頭部的要害處運行（bla dang ‘dra ste ‘khyam pa yi ge’i rnam pa nya stong bcas tshe）。

關於「圖像／示意圖」以及與「五種微細元素——地、水、火、風與空（或本初智慧）」相關的生命氣（風）的運行，於《甚深內義》（The Profound Inner Principles）及其註釋之中，特別是蔣貢康楚（Jamgön Kongtrul）的《甚深內義解脫疏》（Zabmo Nangdön Drelshed，zab mo nang don gyi ‘grel bshad）等論著中有詳盡說明與修持（sādhanā）教導。註釋《甚深內義連結身心明鏡》（Zabmo Nangdön Drelwa’i Lüsem Salwa’i Melong，zab mo nang don ‘brel ba’i lus sems gsal ba’i me long）對此總結如下：

> 「關於風（氣）之運行次第、它們各自的色彩，以及其力量增減的推移：首先，鼻內中央柱（鼻中隔）之區域，對於兩個鼻孔而言被視為北方方位，其中運行的是水大之氣；兩側外緣如翼的部分，是火大之氣運行之處；上方兩段區域，是風大之氣運行之處；下方兩段區域，是地大之氣運行之處；而空與本初智慧之氣，則由兩個鼻孔中央部位運行。
> 
> 更進一步說，鼻中隔的左右兩側內面皆被視為北方，因為：右鼻孔中，與生命氣相連的呼吸，具有透過呼氣向外出離的性質；左鼻孔中，與生命氣相連的呼吸，則具有透過吸氣向內進入的性質。地大之氣的顏色是黃，水大是白，火大是赤，風大是綠或黑，而空大是藍。」

接下來，依據《時輪金剛續》（Kālacakratantra）第二品的說法，內在氣息所經歷的日夜循環，與外在宇宙及其行星的運行周期相類似。最重要的內在循環，就是呼吸本身，因為呼吸與生命氣之間有極為密切的連結——在藏語中兩者皆稱為「隆」（rlung）。在一個以 360 天計算的《時輪金剛》陰曆年中，計有 21,600 個「時刻」。相應地，若我們的生命氣處於平衡狀態，在一晝夜當中，我們的呼吸次數也是 21,600 次。換算成現代時間，大約是每四秒呼吸一次，若我們實際測量自己的呼吸頻率，這個數值相當貼近。

正如太陽在一年之中，有一半時間行於天空的北方區域，有一半時間行於南方區域——天文學上稱為太陽的北行與南行——同樣地，我們也有一半時間主要經由右鼻孔呼吸，而另一半時間主要經由左鼻孔呼吸。

此外，正如太陽在一年之中運行過黃道十二宮，同樣地，呼吸在一晝夜之中，亦於兩側鼻孔之間轉換十二次。也就是說，從右鼻孔開始，伴隨元素氣之呼吸先從地大起，依次運行至水、火、風與空，之後再交替轉換到左鼻孔那一側，由空、風、火、水而至地。這被比擬為行星的「勢力與強度」（stobs dang nu pa）：右鼻孔對應土星、木星、火星、太陽與「黑火」（Kālāgni）；左鼻孔則對應羅睺魔（Rāhu）、月亮、金星、木星與計都（Ketu）。

就修持而言，一切類型的「調息」（prāṇāyāma），尤其是「憋息／壺身息」（kumbakha，藏：bum can、bum pa can），也就是有節制且和諧地，將呼吸與生命氣共同攝持的修法，其關鍵上可分為四種情況：  
（1）呼吸主要在右鼻孔流動時；  
（2）主要在左鼻孔流動時；  
（3）兩鼻孔流動平衡、同量且通暢時；  
（4）完全無流動時。

總的來說，若呼吸以相等的力度同時經由兩鼻孔流出，是生命氣已進入中央脈的徵相。因此，最適合修持的時機，是在呼吸毫無流動，或是和諧地同時流經兩鼻孔的時候。此外，當呼吸主要行於左鼻孔時，尤其吉祥有利。反之，若呼吸只從右鼻孔，或右側佔優勢，則應避免在此時進行修持。替代的作法，是先配合吸氣、憋息與呼氣，行持一些「幻力運動」（‘khrul ‘khor，身體的調身動作），以改變氣流，使其轉向左側，或令兩鼻孔的氣流勢力趨於平衡。

若將手放在鼻下，就很容易驗證：任何時刻，我們多半是以其中一側鼻孔為主在呼吸。只有在氣流由一側轉向另一側的那幾口呼吸之間，氣息才會稍為較均勻地同時經由兩側通過；除此之外，一般並不會同時等量流經兩鼻孔。

Longevity – Tantric and Medicinal Subtle Channels Related to One’s Lifespan and Breathing    
According to both, Tibetan tantric cycles and Traditional Tibetan Medicine (TTM), there are a various tantric and medicinal systems elucidating most subtle channels and capillaries, which spread and diffuse the entire human body like a fine network of minute minor channels (yang phran rtsa’i lus kyi dra bar ‘brel tshul). Those networks of channels derive from the three major subtle channels via the connecting subtle channels of the cakras or channel wheels and interlink the most subtle channels like a minute net. In this respect, the Four Medicine Tantras together with the Blue Beryl define three courses and modes related to one’s longevity or lifespan (tshe):    
1. The mode of lifespan channels (tshe’i rtsa), which pervades the entire physical body and the head in the form of seed-syllables during the days of the month (yi ge’i rnam pa nya stong tshe mgo lus thams cad du khyab pa),    
2. The mode of the vital energies related to the five subtle elements, which move together with one’s respiration (‘byung ba’i rlung dbugs dang ‘grogs te rgyu ba), and    
3. The mode of one’s essential protective energy that moves through the vital points of one’s physical body and the head in the form of seed-syllables during the days of the month (bla dang ‘dra ste ‘khyam pa yi ge’i rnam pa nya stong bcas tshe).    
Regarding the drawing and the vital energies related to the five subtle elements of earth, water, fire, wind, and space or wisdom you find elaborate explanations and practice instructions in The Profound Inner Principles and their commentaries such as Jamgön Kongtrul’s Zabmo Nangdön Drelshed (zab mo nang don gyi ‘grel bshad). The commentary Zabmo Nangdön Drelwa’i Lüsem Salwa’i Melong (zab mo nang don ‘brel ba’i lus sems gsal ba’i me long) summarizes this as follows:    
“Regarding the explanation of the sequence of the winds’ movement, their colors, and the progression of their strengths: First, the area of the nose’s inner central column (nasal septum) represents the northern direction for both nostrils, where the vital energy of the water element moves. The two outer wing-like sides of the nostrils are where the vital energy of the fire element moves. The two upper sections are where the vital energy of the wind element moves. The two lower sections are where the vital energy of the earth element moves. The vital energy of space and primordial wisdom moves through the central part of both nostrils. Furthermore, both inner sides of the nasal septum are considered the northern direction because the respiration together with the vital energy of the right nostril has the nature of exiting externally via one’s exhalation, while the respiration together with the vital energy of the left nostril has the nature of entering internally via one’s inhalation. The color of the vital energy of the earth element is yellow, of water is white, of fire is red, of wind is green or black, and of space is blue.”    
Now, according to the second chapter of the Kālacakratantra the daily cycles through which it passes are analogous to the external cycles of the universe and its planets. The most important internal cycle is that of the breath, since there is an intimate connection between the breath and one’s vital energies, which are both known as lung (rlung) in Tibetan. In a 360-day Kālacakra lunar year, there are 21,600 hours. Similarly, if our vital energies are balanced, we breathe 21,600 times during the course of a day and night. This works out to one breath about every four modern seconds, which is accurate when we time our own respiration. Just as the sun travels half the year through the northern sector of the sky and half through the southern one – known astronomically as the sun's northern and southern declinations – likewise we breathe half the time predominantly through the right nostril and the other half through the left.     
Furthermore, just as the sun travels through the twelve zodiac signs during a year, likewise the breath shifts from one nostril to the other twelve times during a day and night. Thus, the breathing together with the vital energies of the elements from the right nostril begins with earth and moves to water, fire, wind, and space before it alternatingly shifts to the left nostril’s space, wind, fire, water and earth. This is likened to the power and strength (stobs dang nu pa) of the planets Saturn, Jupiter, Mars, Sun, and Kālāgni (right nostril), and Rāhu, Moon, Venus, Jupiter, and Ketu (left nostril).     
As for one’s practice, all kinds of prāṇāyāma and in particular kumbakha (bum can, bum pa can), the controlled and harmonious holding of the breath together with one’s vital energies, four times are distinguished when the breathing is predominant in the right or left nostril, when it is balanced and flowing equally free through both nostrils, and when there is no movement at all. In general, movement through both nostrils with equal force is a sign the vital energies have entered the central channel. Thus, the best time for practice is when there is no movement or when it harmoniously flows through both nostrils. Moreover, it is most favorable when it flows through the left nostril. If the breathing is only via or predominant in the right nostril you should avoid practicing. Instead, apply some illusory movements (‘khrul ‘khor) combined with inhalation, holding one’s breath, and exhalation to change the movement to the left side or equalize the strength of the breath in both nostrils. If we put our hand beneath our nose, you can easily verify that you breathe mainly through one nostril at any time. Except for a few breaths during the shift from one nostril to the other, the breath normally does not pass evenly through both.
