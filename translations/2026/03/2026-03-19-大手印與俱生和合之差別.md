### 標題
**大手印與俱生和合之差別**  
The Difference Between Mahamudra and Co-emergent Union

---

### 藏文問答原文與翻譯

7.【問】：「大手印」與「俱生和合」二者是否有所差異？  
འོ་ན་ཕྱག་རྒྱ་ཆེན་པོ་དང་ལྷན་ཅིག་སྐྱེས་སྦྱོར་གཉིས་ལ་ཁྱད་བདོག་གམ་མི་བདོག་ཞུས་པས།  
譯：那麼，「大手印」與「俱生和合」這二者之間，是否存在差別與否的問題，被提出來請問：

師云：「大手印」乃是輪迴暨涅槃諸法普悉本即任運自成，法性如同虛空，  
ཕྱག་རྒྱ་ཆེན་པོ་ནི་འཁོར་བ་དང་མྱ་ངན་ལས་འདས་པའི་ཆོས་ཐམས་ཅད་ཡེ་ནས་ལྷུན་གྱིས་གྲུབ་པ། ཆོས་ཉིད་ནམ་མཁའ་  
譯：「大手印」是指輪迴與涅槃一切諸法，從本初起就任運自然成就；其法性如同虛空，

一切時之本智為不二而無間斷；「俱生和合」  
ལྟ་བུ་དུས་ཐམས་ཅད་པའི་ཡེ་ཤེས། གཉིས་མེད་ཅིག་ཡིན་ཏེ་རྒྱུན་ཆད་པ་མེད་པ་ཡིན། ལྷན་ཅིག་སྐྱེས་སྦྱོར་ནི་  
譯：如是於一切時中之本初智慧，是不二唯一，且沒有任何間斷。「俱生和合」則是，

乃是隨念所生（悉予）合為四身，不認定為一切時而有間斷。  
རྟོག་པ་གང་སྐྱེས་སྐུ་བཞིར་སྦྱོར་བ་ཡིན་པས་དུས་ཐམས་ཅད་པར་མི་འདོད་དེ་རྒྱུན་ཆད་ཡོད་གསུང༌།  
譯：凡是任何念頭生起時，將之與四身相應和合，因此並不承認是在一切時中皆無間斷，而是承認其中有斷續。

### 中英釋義版（依原文提供內容整理）

**白話翻譯（中文）**：

Q7. 帕摩主巴問：「大手印」與「俱生和合」這兩者，有什麼差別嗎？  

岡波巴上師回答：  
所謂「大手印」，是指輪迴與涅槃的一切現象，本來就自然圓滿具足，不是後來才造作出來的。這種法性的本質，就像虛空一樣，無邊無際、恆常存在。它是一種本來就具足的智慧，沒有對立，也沒有中斷，始終如此。  

而「俱生和合」則是一種修行的方法：當任何念頭生起時，修行者將這些念頭轉化，並認知它們本身就是佛的四身的顯現。  

因此，「大手印」指的是本來就存在、從不間斷的真實本性；而「俱生和合」則是修行時運用念頭來體認這個本性的方式。由於念頭有生有滅，所以這種修持並不是恆常不斷的，而是會有間斷的。

**康祖法王開示節錄（中文）**：

問題：「大手印」與「俱生和合」二者是否有所差異？  

師云：「大手印」乃是輪迴暨涅槃諸法普悉本即任運自成；法性如同虛空，一切時中之本智不二且無有間斷。」  

這是說，大手印的實相本身從無任何中斷。如同所謂「基位無有破立與斷捨」（在本性層面上，沒有建立與破除，也沒有捨棄與斷除），它本來即如是存在，自性清淨，從未間斷，並且於一切時中任運自然顯現，因此說「普悉本即任運自成」（一切本來任運自成）。  

又云：「俱生和合，乃隨念所生而合為四身，不認定為一切時皆無間斷。」  

這是說，「俱生和合」是從補特伽羅（行者）的角度而言，故顯現為似有間斷。正如偈文所說：「心性俱時生起乃法身，境相俱時生起乃法身光，念頭俱時生起乃法身用。」¹ 也就是說，隨著念頭的生起，行者依自身的境界，將其體認為法身、報身、化身等，乃至四身；亦即依自己的證悟與認知，而決定其顯現為三身或四身。這是隨念而生，並由行者加以和合認定，因此稱為「俱生和合」。  

故云：「若悟則是法身，不悟則是念頭；若悟則是本智，不悟則是煩惱；若悟則是涅槃，不悟則是輪迴。」² 因此，「俱生和合」是就行者自身的證悟境界而言，才安立此名，故說「不認定為一切時而無間斷（並不是恆常不斷的，而是會有間斷的）」。  

這與大手印不同。大手印於一切時中本來無間斷地存在，如同太陽恆常安住於虛空之中，不會因烏雲遮蔽與否，而有存在或消失的差別。太陽始終存在，只是是否被見到有所不同。同樣地，對行者而言，雖有念頭生起，亦有是否能將其和合為四身的差別，但心性本身並不因證悟而新生，也不因未證悟而消失。正如太陽並非因晴天而出現，也非因陰天而消失，它本來即恆常存在。

**英文版（原文保留）**：

Q7. Phagmo Drupa asked:  
What is the difference between Mahamudra and Co-emergent Union?

Guru Gampopa replied:  
Mahamudra is the natural, spontaneously accomplished state of all phenomena of samsara and nirvana. The nature of reality is like space, and the primordial wisdom present at all times is nondual and uninterrupted.  

Co-emergent Union is that which, arising together with thoughts, is unified as the four kayas; it is not established as being uninterrupted at all times.  

Teachings of Khamtrul Rinpoche:  
Question: Is there any difference between “Mahamudra” and “Co-emergent Union”?  

The master said:  
“Mahamudra is the natural, spontaneously accomplished state of all phenomena of samsara and nirvana. The nature of reality is like space, and the primordial wisdom present at all times is nondual and uninterrupted.”

## 【附註/名相對照】

- 大手印（mahāmudrā）：此處指輪迴與涅槃一切諸法本來任運自成之實相與教法體系。  
- 俱生和合：隨念頭生起而行者將之認定、和合為三身或四身之修持方式。  
- 四身：法身、報身、化身及自性身（或依不同傳承對四身之解釋略有差異）（待考）。  
- 基位：指本性、本覺之基，未加修持前之本然境界。  
- 補特伽羅：行者、有情個體（pudgala）。  

---

## 【疑點/勘誤】

1. 「悉予」：出現在「隨念所生(悉予)合為四身」一句中，疑為「悉皆」或音譯（待考），原意應作「一切念頭皆」之類義。  
2. 四身具體指涉之第四身是「自性身」或其他稱法，依不同傳承詮釋或有差異（待考），此處未臆造確定說明。

Q7. Phagmo Drupa asked:    
What is the difference between Mahamudra and Co-emergent Union?    
Guru Gampopa replied:    
Mahamudra is the natural, spontaneously accomplished state of all phenomena of samsara and nirvana. The nature of reality is like space, and the primordial wisdom present at all times is nondual and uninterrupted.    
Co-emergent Union is that which, arising together with thoughts, is unified as the four kayas; it is not established as being uninterrupted at all times.    
Teachings of Khamtrul Rinpoche:    
Question: Is there any difference between “Mahamudra” and “Co-emergent Union”?    
The master said:    
“Mahamudra is the natural, spontaneously accomplished state of all phenomena of samsara and nirvana. The nature of reality is like space, and the primordial wisdom present at all times is nondual and uninterrupted.”    
This means that the true nature of Mahamudra itself is never interrupted. As it is said, “At the level of the ground, there is nothing to abandon or establish.” It has always been present in this way. Its nature is primordially pure and has never been interrupted. It naturally and spontaneously manifests at all times. Therefore, it is said that “all is originally and spontaneously accomplished.”    
It is also said:    
“Co-emergent Union is that which, arising together with thoughts, is unified as the four kayas; it is not established as being uninterrupted at all times.”    
This means that Co-emergent Union is explained from the perspective of the individual practitioner (pudgala), and thus appears to involve interruption. As stated in the verse:

“When mind-nature arises simultaneously, it is the Dharmakaya.    
When appearances arise simultaneously, they are the radiance of the Dharmakaya.    
When thoughts arise simultaneously, they are the expressive power of the Dharmakaya.”    
In other words, as thoughts arise, the practitioner, according to their own level of realization, unifies them as Dharmakaya, Sambhogakaya, Nirmanakaya, or the four kayas. Based on one’s own recognition and realization, one determines whether they are the three kayas or four kayas. Since this depends on thoughts arising and the practitioner recognizing and integrating them, it is called “Co-emergent Union.”    
Thus it is said:    
“If realized, it is the Dharmakaya; if not realized, it is merely thoughts.    
If realized, it is primordial wisdom; if not realized, it is affliction.    
If realized, it is nirvana; if not realized, it is samsara.”    
Therefore, “Co-emergent Union” is designated from the standpoint of the practitioner’s own realization. For this reason, it is said that it is not established as uninterrupted at all times.    
This is unlike Mahamudra. Mahamudra exists uninterruptedly at all times. It is like the sun abiding in the sky—it does not come into existence or cease to exist depending on whether clouds obscure it. The sun itself is always present; only whether it is seen or not differs.    
Likewise, although thoughts arise for the practitioner, and there is a difference in whether they are recognized and unified as the four kayas, the nature of mind itself does not arise upon realization, nor does it disappear when not realized. Just as the sun does not appear only on clear days or disappear on cloudy days, it has always been present from the very beginning.
