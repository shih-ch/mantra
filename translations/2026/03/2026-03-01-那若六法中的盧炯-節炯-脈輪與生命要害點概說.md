## 【正文】

Six Yogas of Nāropā – Lüjong, Tsigjong, Cakras and Marmas According to the Nāropādaṣaḍdharma  
那若六法——依《那若巴六法》（Nāropādaṣaḍdharma）所說之盧炯、節炯、輪與生命要害點

Rare drawings and details concerning Nāro Chödrug’s subtle anatomy of cakras and marmas.  
關於那若六法（Nāro Chödrug）中脈輪與生命要害點（marmas）之微細身解剖的珍稀圖像與細節。

According to the famous Six Yogas of Naropa known in Tibetan as Nāro Chödrug (nA ra chos drug) the practitioner - male and female – manifests in the empty dimension of Vajrayogini (rdo rje 'phag mo’i stong ra) by visualizing him- or herself as female deity.  
依據廣為人知、藏語稱為「那若六法」（Nāro Chödrug, nA ra chos drug）的那若六法，行者——無論男眾女眾——皆於金剛瑜伽母（Vajrayoginī，rdo rje 'phag mo’i stong ra）之空性界中，以觀想自成為女尊的方式而顯現。

Thus, both male and female practitioners transform into a female manifestation where the subtle channels and the major cakras and and minor marmas appear in the corresponding manner.  
因此，男女行者皆轉為一位女性本尊之相，於其中其微細氣脈，以及諸大脈輪與支分生命要害點，依相應方式呈現。

Depending on the particular practice such as the practice of inner heat and pleasurable sensation,, tummo (gtum mo/caṇḍālī) or consciousness transference, phowa (‘pho ba/saṃkrānti), the practitioner may visualize either four, five, or six cakras or channel wheels ('khor lo, rtsa ‘khor/cakra), where the red, solar, and female channel on the right side and the white, lunar, and male channel on the left side coil around the central channel at each wheel once and at the heart wheel three times, or where all three main channels may remain straight from the bottom at the secret cakra to the top of the head.  
視具體修法而定，例如內熱（caṇḍālī）與悅意感受之修習——拙火（tummo，gtum mo/caṇḍālī），或識轉移——頗瓦（phowa，‘pho ba/saṃkrānti）等，行者可觀想四、五或六個脈輪或氣脈輪（'khor lo，rtsa ‘khor/cakra）。此時，右側紅色、屬日、屬女之脈，以及左側白色、屬月、屬男之脈，在各個輪處環繞中脈（central channel）一匝，而在心輪處則環繞三匝；又或者，三條主脈自下方祕密輪處直至頭頂處皆保持筆直不曲。

Moreover, additional to those main channel wheels or cakras (‘khor lo chen, rtsa ‘khor chen/cakra) there are twelve major and sixty-four minor channel wheels of one’s physical joints, which partially correspond to vital, vulnerable, and sensitive energy points known in Sanskrit as marmas and in Tibetan as ‘essential points of one’s subtle channels’, ne or tsane (gnas, rtsa gnas/marma).  
此外，除那些主氣脈輪或脈輪（'khor lo chen，rtsa ‘khor chen/cakra）以外，於行者身體關節處尚有十二大與六十四支分脈輪，它們部分對應於梵語所稱之生命要害點（marmas），藏語稱為「細脈要點」、即細脈之要害點——ne 或 tsane（gnas, rtsa gnas/marma）。

As those essential points and their subtle channels correspond to the location of the joints of the body they are also referred to as lükyi tsig (lus kyi tshigs/kāyaparvan).  
由於這些要點及其微細氣脈對應於身體各關節之位置，故亦稱為「身之節」（lükyi tsig，lus kyi tshigs/kāya-parvan）。

According to Tibetan medicine such as such as The Four Medical Tantras (rgyud bzhi), the physical body contains twelve major joints and three-hundred sixty or sixty-four minor joints, often treated to relieve arthritis (grum bu) and enhance mobility.  
依《四部醫典》（The Four Medical Tantras，rgyud bzhi）等藏醫典籍所說，肉身之中具十二大關節與三百六十或六十四個小關節，醫療上常予以調治以減輕關節病（grum bu），並增進靈活度。

Generally, the body’s twelve major joints are the two shoulders, two elbows, two wrists, two hips, two knees, and two ankles, which are likewise depicted according to other systems as such of the Kalacakratantra.  
一般而言，身體之十二大關節為兩肩、兩肘、兩腕、兩髖、兩膝與兩踝；依其他系統（如《時輪金剛密續》Kalacakratantra）亦作如是描繪。

Referring to the anatomical classification of one’s physical body's smaller joints and one’s subtle body’s energetical points, where certain subtle channels transporting vital energies (rlung/prāṇavāyu) and vital essences (thig le/ śuklabodhicitta) meet, several traditions such as the Shangpa Kagyüd include them in their energetical training of tsalung trulkhor.  
就肉身較小關節與細身能量點之解剖分類而言，於某些細脈之中，運載命氣（rlung/prāṇavāyu）與精要（thig le/śuklabodhicitta）之微細氣脈在彼處交會；數個傳承，例如香巴噶舉（Shangpa Kagyüd），即將這些部位納入其氣與拙火、壯行修法（tsalung trulkhor）之能量訓練之中。

In this respect the teachings and the yogic anatomical charts state:  
在此方面，教誡與瑜伽解剖掛圖中如是說明：

“The twelve major joints each have eight channel petals,  
「十二大關節各具八瓣氣脈之瓣葉，

and the sixty-four minor joints each have four channel petals.”  
而六十四小關節各具四瓣氣脈之瓣葉。」

Moreover, the recent drawing clarifies:  
此外，較新的圖像更進一步闡明：

“In the context of The Six Yogas of Nāropa, it is taught that since the channels of the hands branch out from the channel-wheel at the level of the heart, i.e. dharma-cakra, one should meditate on them as being white, matching the color of the heart cakra.  
「在那若六法之脈輪系統中，教示云：由於雙手之氣脈自心之層位之脈輪分支而出，即法輪（dharma-cakra），故應觀想其為白色，以與心輪之色相相合。

Similarly, because the channels of the feet branch out from the channel-wheel at the level of the navel, i.e. the nirmāṇa-cakra, one should meditate on them as being red, in accordance with the color of the navel cakra.”  
同樣地，由於雙足之氣脈自臍之層位之脈輪分支而出，即變化輪（nirmāṇa-cakra），故應觀想其為紅色，以與臍輪之顏色相應。」

Regarding the sixty-four minor joints, while one of the versions of the older black & white drawing depicts each 15 vital points (5 x 3) at the two hands and fingers and each 14 vital points (4 x 3 + 1 x 2) at the two feet and toes, making 58, the recent colored version depicts each 15 vital points (5 x 3) at the hands and fingers, which depart from each one additional vital point at the lower part of the wrist joint (carpal) and the lower point of the ankle joint (tarsus), altogether 64.  
就六十四個小關節而論，較舊的黑白圖像之其中一種版本，是於雙手與指頭各示一十五個要害點（5 × 3），於雙足與趾頭各示一十四個要害點（4 × 3 + 1 × 2），合計為五十八個；而較新的彩色版本則於雙手與指頭各示一十五個要害點（5 × 3），並於腕關節下部（腕骨）及踝關節下部（跗骨）各增列一個要害點，總計為六十四個。

Thus, the older version may refer to additional joints at the head and vertebrae.  
因此，較舊版本或許是將頭部與椎骨處的額外關節一併計入（待考）。

As the sixty-four minor joints enhance mobility the Tibetan Yogas of Movement at times dedicate certain illusory movements or trulkhor in their systems.  
由於六十四個小關節能增進身體活動度，故在藏傳「運動之瑜伽」中，時常於其體系內安置特定之幻化運動或壯行（trulkhor）。

In contrast to most other illusory magical movements (‘khrul ‘khor, ‘phrul ‘khor/yantra) those trulkhor or yantra are sometimes referred to as lüjong (lus sbyong), ‘physical training’ or ‘body purification’ and even as tsigjong (tshigs sbyong), ‘training of one’s body’s joints.  
與大多數其他幻化神通動作（'khrul 'khor, 'phrul 'khor/yantra）相較之下，這些壯行或 yantra 有時被稱為「盧炯」（lüjong，lus sbyong），意為「身體訓練」或「身體清淨」，甚至稱為「節炯」（tsigjong，tshigs sbyong），意為「身之節（關節）之訓練」。

For example, in Chögyal Namkhai Norbu’s Yantra Yoga the first preliminary series of trulkhor are known as Five Tsigjong (tshigs sbyong gi bye brag mam pa lnga), where the Tibetan word Tsigjong is a compound consisting of tsig, meaning ‘joints,’ and jong, ‘training.’  
例如，在卻嘉·南開諾布（Chögyal Namkhai Norbu）所傳之壯行瑜伽（Yantra Yoga）中，第一個預備系列的壯行稱為「五節炯」（Five Tsigjong，tshigs sbyong gi bye brag mam pa lnga）；其中，藏語「tsigjong」一詞為合成語，由「tsig」，意為「關節」，以及「jong」，意為「訓練」，二者組成。

According to Tibetan medicine, the twelve major joints and 360 or 64 minor joints in the body, with their predominant elements being earth and water, are energized by the subtle movement of vital energies within the center of the articulations.  
依藏醫所說，身中十二大關節與三百六十或六十四個小關節，其主導之五大為地大與水大，並由位於關節中央之命氣（vital energies）微細流動所賦予活力。

In Yantra Yoga practice, those tsigjong are considered as ‘warm-ups’ for both the body and the breathing, creating a foundation for the subsequent practices.  
於壯行瑜伽之修持中，這些節炯被視為身體與呼吸之「熱身」，為後續諸修法奠定基礎。

Instead, in some Six Yogas of Nāropā manuals various illusory movements are applied to enhance one’s yogic practices of inner heat and pleasurable sensation as well as sexual union.  
另一方面，在某些那若六法的修持手冊中，則運用多種幻化運動以強化行者於內熱與悅意感受，以及雙運修持方面的瑜伽實修。

The commonly in the Gelugpa tradition taught ‘six illusory movements’ as outlined in Phagmo Drugpa's text entitled as Treasury of Precious Treasures as Verses of the Path of Skillful Means: A Supplement (thabs lam tshigs bcad ma'i lhan thabs rin chen gter mdzod) states:  
格魯派傳統中常教導之「六種幻化運動」，如帕木竹巴（Phagmo Drupa）之著作《方便道偈頌之補編——珍寶寶藏》（Treasury of Precious Treasures as Verses of the Path of Skillful Means: A Supplement，thabs lam tshigs bcad ma'i lhan thabs rin chen gter mdzod）中所述如下：

“There are six exercises for purifying the body: filling like a vase; circling like a wheel; hooking like a hook; showing the mudra of vajra binding, lifting upward toward the sky, and then pressing downward; straightening like an arrow, and then forcefully re-leasing the air in the manner of a dog heaving; and, in order to energize the passageways and blood in the body, shaking the head and entire body and flexing the muscles. These are the six.”  
「有六種清淨身之鍛鍊：如瓶一般充滿；如輪一般旋轉；如鉤一般鉤牽；結金剛縛印，向上舉至虛空，復又向下按壓；如箭一般伸直，隨後以犬喘之狀強力排出氣息；並且，為了激發身中通道與血液，搖動頭部與全身，並曲伸筋肉。此即六種。」

According to Je Tsongkhapa, these six illusory movements related to the complete holding of one’s vital energies together with the breath (bum can/kumbhaka) have to be done in conjunction with the hollow body contemplation, i.e. the deity’s empty dimension.  
依宗喀巴大師之說，這六種幻化運動與圓滿攝持命氣與呼吸的閉息（bum can/kumbhaka）有關，必須與空身觀——亦即本尊之空性界——相結合而行。

Based on various original texts by Marpa Lotsawa most Kagyüd traditions follow a later practice manual for the actual practice of trulkhor.  
依據瑪爾巴譯師（Marpa Lotsawa）所傳多部原典，多數噶舉傳承在實修壯行時是遵循一部後出的修持儀軌。

This text is called The All-Illuminating Mirror, the Oral Instructions of the Illusory Movements for Training the Body in Order to Progress in the Practice of the Fierce Lady (gtum mo) from Among the Six Yogas of Naropa (nA ro chos drug las gtum mo'i bogs 'don lus sbyong 'phrul 'khor gyi zhal khrid kun gsal me long).  
此文獻名為《普照之鏡》（The All-Illuminating Mirror），全名為《那若六法中猛烈女尊（拙火，gtum mo）進修之身體訓練幻化運動口訣——普照明鏡》（nA ro chos drug las gtum mo'i bogs 'don lus sbyong 'phrul 'khor gyi zhal khrid kun gsal me long）。

There are several introductory Trulkhor which are:  
其中有幾個導入性的壯行，如下所列：

1. The illusory movement for purifying the stains of the vital eneregies /rlung skyon sel ba'i 'phrul 'khor),  
1. 清淨命氣染污之幻化運動（rlung skyon sel ba'i 'phrul 'khor），  

2. The Nine Purification Breathing for Expelling the stale vital energies (rlung ro dgu phrugs 'bud pa),  
2. 驅除陳舊命氣之九種清淨呼吸（rlung ro dgu phrugs 'bud pa），  

3. The five branch trulkhor for cleansing the subtle channels (rtsa khrus yan lag lnga sbyong),  
3. 清洗細脈之五支壯行（rtsa khrus yan lag lnga sbyong），  

4. The (introductory) cycle of the six trulkhor of Naropa (nA ro drug skor),  
4. 那若六種壯行之（入門）輪次（nA ro drug skor），  

5. The (visualizations and the) thirty-seven trulkhor according to Karma Pakshi, (the Second Karmapa) (karma pakshi'i so bdun).  
5. 依據噶瑪巴噶瑪巴希（Karma Pakshi，第二世噶瑪巴）所傳之（觀想及）三十七種壯行（karma pakshi'i so bdun）。

In a video clip Chenga Rinpoche shows the series 2.-5. starting (2:17 min).  
於某段影片中，千噶仁波切（Chenga Rinpoche）自約 2:17 分處開始示現第 2 至第 5 系列。

Finally, the series of Illusory Movements concludes with some so-called bebs.  
最後，幻化運動之整個系列以若干所謂「貝」（bebs，受身跌落動作）作為結尾。

In Tibetan Yoga or Trulkor there are controlled jumps and falls ('bebs) for opening the body's subtle channels (rtsa) so that vital energies (rlung) move coordinated and harmoniously through the subtle channels in order to promote higher states of awareness.  
在藏傳瑜伽或壯行（Trulkor）之中，存在一些受控制之跳躍與跌落（'bebs），藉以開啟身體之細脈（rtsa），使命氣（rlung）得以協調而和諧地於細脈中運行，以促進較高之覺知狀態。

Those don here are an impressive demonstration by Chenga Rinpoche (spyan snga rin po che) from the Drikung Kaggyü ('bri kung bka' brgyud) tradition.  
此處所示者，即屬直貢噶舉（Drikung Kagyü，'bri kung bka' brgyud）傳承之千噶仁波切（Chenga Rinpoche，spyan snga rin po che）所做出之極具震撼力的示範。

These Trulkhor ('phrul 'khor) or Yantras are found in the Tibetan text of the Oral Tradition of Cakrasambhava or Demchog (bde mchog snyan rgyud kyi rtsa rlung 'khrul 'khor gyi skor).  
這些壯行（Trulkhor，'phrul 'khor）或 yantra，可見於《勝樂金剛口傳》（Cakrasambhava 或德宮——Demchog，bde mchog snyan rgyud kyi rtsa rlung 'khrul 'khor gyi skor）之藏文中所載之氣脈壯行部份。

## 【偈頌/祈願文】

1. “The twelve major joints each have eight channel petals,  
   轉寫：—  
   漢音：—  
   繁體中文翻譯：「十二大關節各具八瓣氣脈之瓣葉，  
   句義：說明身體十二大關節，各自具備八瓣氣脈瓣葉之結構。

2. and the sixty-four minor joints each have four channel petals.”  
   轉寫：—  
   漢音：—  
   繁體中文翻譯：而六十四小關節各具四瓣氣脈之瓣葉。」  
   句義：說明六十四個小關節，各自具備四瓣氣脈瓣葉。

3. “In the context of The Six Yogas of Nāropa, it is taught that since the channels of the hands branch out from the channel-wheel at the level of the heart, i.e. dharma-cakra, one should meditate on them as being white, matching the color of the heart cakra.  
   轉寫：—  
   漢音：—  
   繁體中文翻譯：「在那若六法的脈輪系統中，教示云：由於雙手之氣脈自心之脈輪層位分枝而出，即法輪（dharma-cakra），故應觀想其為白色，以與心輪之色相合。  
   句義：解說雙手氣脈的出處與顏色觀想——由心輪法輪分出，應觀為白色。

4. Similarly, because the channels of the feet branch out from the channel-wheel at the level of the navel, i.e. the nirmāṇa-cakra, one should meditate on them as being red, in accordance with the color of the navel cakra.”  
   轉寫：—  
   漢音：—  
   繁體中文翻譯：同樣地，由於雙足之氣脈自臍之脈輪層位分枝而出，即變化輪（nirmāṇa-cakra），故應觀想其為紅色，以與臍輪之顏色相應。」  
   句義：說明雙足氣脈自臍輪變化輪分出，觀想為紅色。

5. “There are six exercises for purifying the body: filling like a vase; circling like a wheel; hooking like a hook; showing the mudra of vajra binding, lifting upward toward the sky, and then pressing downward; straightening like an arrow, and then forcefully re-leasing the air in the manner of a dog heaving; and, in order to energize the passageways and blood in the body, shaking the head and entire body and flexing the muscles. These are the six.”  
   轉寫：—  
   漢音：—  
   繁體中文翻譯：「有六種清淨身之鍛鍊：如瓶一般充滿；如輪一般旋轉；如鉤一般鉤牽；結金剛縛印，向上舉至虛空，復又向下按壓；如箭一般伸直，隨後以犬喘之狀強力排出氣息；並且，為了激發身中通道與血液，搖動頭部與全身，並曲伸筋肉。此即六種。」  
   句義：概述「六種幻化運動」之名稱與作用，著重於清淨身體與激發氣血。

Six Yogas of Nāropā – Lüjong, Tsigjong, Cakras and Marmas According to the Nāropādaṣaḍdharma     
Rare drawings and details concerning Nāro Chödrug’s subtle anatomy of cakras and marmas.    
According to the famous Six Yogas of Naropa known in Tibetan as Nāro Chödrug (nA ra chos drug) the practitioner - male and female – manifests in the empty dimension of Vajrayogini (rdo rje 'phag mo’i stong ra) by visualizing him- or herself as female deity. Thus, both male and female practitioners transform into a female manifestation where the subtle channels and the major cakras and and minor marmas appear in the corresponding manner.     
Depending on the particular practice such as the practice of inner heat and pleasurable sensation,, tummo (gtum mo/caṇḍālī) or consciousness transference, phowa (‘pho ba/saṃkrānti), the practitioner may visualize either four, five, or six cakras or channel wheels ('khor lo, rtsa ‘khor/cakra), where the red, solar, and female channel on the right side and the white, lunar, and male channel on the left side coil around the central channel at each wheel once and at the heart wheel three times, or where all three main channels may remain straight from the bottom at the secret cakra to the top of the head.     
Moreover, additional to those main channel wheels or cakras (‘khor lo chen, rtsa ‘khor chen/cakra) there are twelve major and sixty-four minor channel wheels of one’s physical joints, which partially correspond to vital, vulnerable, and sensitive energy points known in Sanskrit as marmas and in Tibetan as ‘essential points of one’s subtle channels’, ne or tsane (gnas, rtsa gnas/marma). As those essential points and their subtle channels correspond to the location of the joints of the body they are also referred to as lükyi tsig (lus kyi tshigs/kāyaparvan).     
According to Tibetan medicine such as such as The Four Medical Tantras (rgyud bzhi), the physical body contains twelve major joints and three-hundred sixty or sixty-four minor joints, often treated to relieve arthritis (grum bu) and enhance mobility. Generally, the body’s twelve major joints are the two shoulders, two elbows, two wrists, two hips, two knees, and two ankles, which are likewise depicted according to other systems as such of the Kalacakratantra. Referring to the anatomical classification of one’s physical body's smaller joints and one’s subtle body’s energetical points, where certain subtle channels transporting vital energies (rlung/prāṇavāyu) and vital essences (thig le/ śuklabodhicitta) meet, several traditions such as the Shangpa Kagyüd include them in their energetical training of tsalung trulkhor.    
In this respect the teachings and the yogic anatomical charts state:     
“The twelve major joints each have eight channel petals,     
and the sixty-four minor joints each have four channel petals.”     
Moreover, the recent drawing clarifies:    
“In the context of The Six Yogas of Nāropa, it is taught that since the channels of the hands branch out from the channel-wheel at the level of the heart, i.e. dharma-cakra, one should meditate on them as being white, matching the color of the heart cakra. Similarly, because the channels of the feet branch out from the channel-wheel at the level of the navel, i.e. the nirmāṇa-cakra, one should meditate on them as being red, in accordance with the color of the navel cakra.”    
Regarding the sixty-four minor joints, while one of the versions of the older black & white drawing depicts each 15 vital points (5 x 3) at the two hands and fingers and each 14 vital points (4 x 3 + 1 x 2) at the two feet and toes, making 58, the recent colored version depicts each 15 vital points (5 x 3) at the hands and fingers, which depart from each one additional vital point at the lower part of the wrist joint (carpal) and the lower point of the ankle joint (tarsus), altogether 64. Thus, the older version may refer to additional joints at the head and vertebrae.     
As the sixty-four minor joints enhance mobility the Tibetan Yogas of Movement at times dedicate certain illusory movements or trulkhor in their systems. In contrast to most other illusory magical movements (‘khrul ‘khor, ‘phrul ‘khor/yantra) those trulkhor or yantra are sometimes referred to as lüjong (lus sbyong), ‘physical training’ or ‘body purification’ and even as tsigjong (tshigs sbyong), ‘training of one’s body’s joints. For example, in Chögyal Namkhai Norbu’s Yantra Yoga the first preliminary series of trulkhor are known as Five Tsigjong (tshigs sbyong gi bye brag mam pa lnga), where the Tibetan word Tsigjong is a compound consisting of tsig, meaning ‘joints,’ and jong, ‘training.’ According to Tibetan medicine, the twelve major joints and 360 or 64 minor joints in the body, with their predominant elements being earth and water, are energized by the subtle movement of vital energies within the center of the articulations. In Yantra Yoga practice, those tsigjong are considered as ‘warm-ups’ for both the body and the breathing, creating a foundation for the subsequent practices.    
Instead, in some Six Yogas of Nāropā manuals various illusory movements are applied to enhance one’s yogic practices of inner heat and pleasurable sensation as well as sexual union. The commonly in the Gelugpa tradition taught ‘six illusory movements’ as outlined in Phagmo Drugpa's text entitled as Treasury of Precious Treasures as Verses of the Path of Skillful Means: A Supplement (thabs lam tshigs bcad ma'i lhan thabs rin chen gter mdzod) states:    
“There are six exercises for purifying the body: filling like a vase; circling like a wheel; hooking like a hook; showing the mudra of vajra binding, lifting upward toward the sky, and then pressing downward; straightening like an arrow, and then forcefully re-leasing the air in the manner of a dog heaving; and, in order to energize the passageways and blood in the body, shaking the head and entire body and flexing the muscles. These are the six.”    
According to Je Tsongkhapa, these six illusory movements related to the complete holding of one’s vital energies together with the breath (bum can/kumbhaka) have to be done in conjunction with the hollow body contemplation, i.e. the deity’s empty dimension.     
Based on various original texts by Marpa Lotsawa most Kagyüd traditions follow a later practice manual for the actual practice of trulkhor. This text is called The All-Illuminating Mirror, the Oral Instructions of the Illusory Movements for Training the Body in Order to Progress in the Practice of the Fierce Lady (gtum mo) from Among the Six Yogas of Naropa (nA ro chos drug las gtum mo'i bogs 'don lus sbyong 'phrul 'khor gyi zhal khrid kun gsal me long).    
There are several introductory Trulkhor which are:    
1. The illusory movement for purifying the stains of the vital eneregies /rlung skyon sel ba'i 'phrul 'khor),    
2. The Nine Purification Breathing for Expelling the stale vital energies (rlung ro dgu phrugs 'bud pa),    
3. The five branch trulkhor for cleansing the subtle channels (rtsa khrus yan lag lnga sbyong),    
4. The (introductory) cycle of the six trulkhor of Naropa (nA ro drug skor),    
5. The (visualizations and the) thirty-seven trulkhor according to Karma Pakshi, (the Second Karmapa) (karma pakshi'i so bdun).    
In a video clip Chenga Rinpoche shows the series 2.-5. starting (2:17 min). Finally, the series of Illusory Movements concludes with some so-called bebs.    
In Tibetan Yoga or Trulkor there are controlled jumps and falls ('bebs) for opening the body's subtle channels (rtsa) so that vital energies (rlung) move coordinated and harmoniously through the subtle channels in order to promote higher states of awareness. Those don here are an impressive demonstration by Chenga Rinpoche (spyan snga rin po che) from the Drikung Kaggyü ('bri kung bka' brgyud) tradition. These Trulkhor ('phrul 'khor) or Yantras are found in the Tibetan text of the Oral Tradition of Cakrasambhava or Demchog (bde mchog snyan rgyud kyi rtsa rlung 'khrul 'khor gyi skor).
