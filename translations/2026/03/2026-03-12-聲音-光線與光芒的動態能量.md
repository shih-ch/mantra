## 【正文】

**Dynamic Energy of Sounds, Rays and Lights**  
聲音、光線與光芒的動態能量

在大圓滿（大圓滿法，dzogpachenpo）的脈絡中說明到：聲音、光與光芒是由空性中生起，而由於未能認識它們的真實本性，便凝結成為五種物質元素。

因此，一切所感知到的現象——無論是清淨的見（淨相）或不清淨的業力見——都被視為自心覺性之能量（rig pa'i rtsal）在外在空間中的動態顯現，是自己潛能（rang rtsal）向外「射出／投射」的展現。

大圓滿的「透空虹光」（Thögal，穿越法）是建立在此理解之上，並藉由對聲音、光線與光芒的「聽聞與見相修持」的實修運用而展開。這三者稱為「聲、光、光芒三」（dra özer sum，sgra 'od zer gsum），即個人「三種潛能」或動態能量的三個面向。它們被理解為自身覺性（rig pa）能量自然且自發的顯現，向外顯現為所感知的宇宙與眾生——能所二分中的「器世間與有情世間」——以及種種感官所現的相狀，如透空虹光修持中所出現的諸種幻相與見相。

在某種意義上，「聲」（sgra）可指「聲之秘密處」或「覺性之聲」（rig pa'i sgra），常與空性的本然之聲相關，例如當以手掩耳而聆聽自己內在之聲時所聞之聲。故此，也被稱為「本初之聲」或「阿字之始聲」，為一切光明顯現與見相之基礎。

「光」（'od）是指心之本性自生、自成、清淨而光明的本然狀態，於修持中常被經驗為五色光明球、圓形之滴（thigle）、影像或壇城等。

「光芒」（zer）則是從這些光明顯相中輻射、發散並繁衍出去的光線與光束，構成透空虹光之「四種見」的一部分。

Dynamic Energy of Sounds, Rays and Lights    
In the context of dzogpachenpo, it is explained that sound, light, and rays arise from emptiness and, through the lack of recognition of their true nature, solidify into the five material elements.     
Thus, all perceived phenomena - whether pure visions or impure karmic visions - are considered the display of the dynamic energy of one's own awareness (rig pa'i rtsal) in external space, a 'projection' of one's potentiality (rang rtsal).    
Dzogchen Thögal is based on the understanding and experiential application of the auditary and visionary practices with sounds, rays, and lights, dra özer sum (sgra 'od zer gsum), the three aspects of one's 'three potentialities' or dynamic energies. These are understood as the natural, spontaneous display of the energy of one's own Awareness (rig pa), which manifests outwardly as the perceived universe and beings, container and contained, and one's sensory appearances such as thögal visions.     
In a sense sound (sgra) may refer to the "secret place of sound" or "sound of rigpa" (rig pa'i sgra), often related to the innate sound of emptiness, such as the sound heard when blocking the ears for listening to one's inner sound. Thus, it is also known as primordial sound or alpha sound of A, the base for luminous manifestations and visions.    
Lights ('od) 'refers to the self-arising, pristine, and luminous nature of the mind, often experienced as five-colored luminous spheres or circle-like thigles, images, or mandalas during practice.    
Rays (zer) are the issuing rays or beams of light that radiate and profiliorate from these luminous visions as part of thögal's four visions.
