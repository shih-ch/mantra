## 【正文】

經過多年修持，女子益西措嘉（Yeshe Tsogyal）仍不斷被欲望所擾。她在蓮師（Guru Rinpoche）前頂禮伏地，哭泣著說：「上師，我每日勤奮持守戒律，卻仍無法擺脫欲望的枷鎖。我是否永遠不能得到解脫？」  

蓮師並未開示說法，只是點亮一盞酥油燈，遞給她：「為什麼這火焰會燃燒得如此明亮？」措嘉回答：「因為燈芯與酥油融合。」  

蓮師接著問：「若是把酥油去掉呢？」  

她答道：「那就自然熄滅了。」  

聞言，蓮師笑道：「你為何要試圖把自己的欲望拔除、將之熄滅，而不是把它們轉為光明呢？」  

措嘉一時困惑——在她的理解中，欲望是修持的大敵，必須斬斷才能得到解脫。  

蓮師隨後遞給她一面銅鏡：「情緒如同暴風。如果你逃避它們，它們就會變得更為強烈。唯有進入風暴的中心，才能見到它的真實性即是空性。用這面鏡子來觀照自心，讓你的欲望自然顯現，不要抗拒也不要壓抑。你將會看到，它們不過是空無的雲朵，一旦雲散，本具的光明便會顯露。」  

依照上師的教誡，措嘉起初仍與自己的欲望對抗，直到筋疲力盡時，才憶念起上師的教言。她安坐禪修，觀察自己的欲望如潮水般湧來，卻不再執著於「根除」它們的念頭，只是如旁觀者般，默然觀照它們的起伏往還。

After years of spiritual practice, Yeshe Tsogyal was constantly tormented by desire. She prostrated herself before Guru Rinpoche and wept, "Master, I diligently uphold my precepts every day, yet I cannot escape the shackles of desire. Am I forever unable to attain liberation?" The Guru did not preach; he simply lit a butter lamp and handed it to her. "Why does this flame burn so brightly?" Tsogyal answered, "Because the wick and the butter merge." The Guru then asked, "What if you were to remove the butter?" "It would naturally extinguish," she replied. Hearing this, the Guru laughed and said, "Why do you try to remove your desires and extinguish them, rather than transform them into light?" Tsogyal was bewildered—for in her understanding, desire was the great enemy of practice, something that had to be severed to achieve liberation. The Guru then handed her a bronze mirror. "Emotions are like a storm. If you flee from them, they grow stronger. Only by entering the eye of the storm can you see that its true nature is emptiness. Use the mirror to observe your mind. Allow your desires to manifest naturally, without resistance or suppression. You will see they are but empty clouds, and when they dissipate, the inherent light within will be revealed." Following his advice, Tsogyal at first still fought against her desires, until exhausted, she remembered her master's teachings. She sat in meditation, observing as her desires surged in like a tide, but no longer clinging to the idea of 'eradicating' them. She merely watched their ebb and flow as a detached observer.
